## 1. Introduction

This document records the usability evaluation conducted on the Tsala Market user interface prototype.

The purpose of the evaluation is to determine whether users can understand and successfully complete important tasks using the proposed system.

The evaluation focuses on identifying usability problems, observing user interactions, evaluating the clarity of navigation and interface instructions, and identifying improvements that can be made before implementation.

The usability evaluation involves three participants who are asked to complete selected tasks using the Tsala Market prototype.

The findings will be used to revise the interface design and improve the overall usability of the system.

## 2. Objectives of the Usability Test

The usability test aims to:

1. Determine whether users can navigate the Tsala Market interface without unnecessary assistance.
2. Evaluate whether farmers can successfully use the Check My Offer functionality.
3. Evaluate whether users understand the displayed market-price comparison results.
4. Determine whether vendors can submit agricultural price information correctly.
5. Identify confusing interface elements, unclear instructions and unnecessary steps.
6. Evaluate whether error messages and validation feedback are understandable.
7. Identify accessibility and usability improvements.

## 3. Target Users and Participants

The evaluation involves three participants representing potential users of the Tsala Market system.

| Participant | User Perspective | Description |

## 4. Test Environment and Methodology

### 4.1 Test Environment

The evaluation will be conducted using the Tsala Market prototype.

The following resources will be required:

- A computer or mobile device.
- The Tsala Market wireframes or clickable prototype.
- A test scenario for each participant.
- A means of recording observations.
- A stopwatch or timer to record task completion time, if available.

### 4.2 Testing Method

A task-based usability evaluation will be used.

Each participant will receive a realistic scenario and will be asked to complete the specified task using the prototype.

The evaluator will observe the participant without unnecessarily guiding them through the interface.

The evaluator will record:

- Whether the participant completed the task.
- The time taken to complete the task.
- Any navigation difficulties.
- Errors or incorrect selections.
- Requests for assistance.
- Participant comments and suggestions.

The evaluator should encourage participants to explain what they expect to happen as they interact with the interface.

### 4.3 Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| Task Completion | Whether the participant successfully completed the assigned task. |
| Ease of Navigation | Whether the participant could locate the required functionality. |
| Understandability | Whether instructions, labels and results were easy to understand. |
| Error Recovery | Whether the participant could identify and recover from errors. |
| Efficiency | Whether the task could be completed without unnecessary steps. |
| Accessibility | Whether interface elements were readable, identifiable and easy to interact with. |

## 5. Usability Test Scenarios and Tasks

### Task 1: Check My Offer (UC-01)

**Target User:** Smallholder Farmer

**Scenario:**

A farmer in the Kweneng District wants to sell tomatoes. A buyer offers P152 for a 20 kg crate of tomatoes. The farmer wants to determine whether the offer is reasonable compared with the available verified market prices.

**Participant Instructions:**

Using the Tsala Market interface:

1. Open the Check My Offer functionality.
2. Select Tomatoes.
3. Select Kweneng District.
4. Select the appropriate price unit.
5. Enter the offered price.
6. Submit the information.
7. Interpret the displayed fairness classification and market reference.

**Success Criteria:**

The participant should be able to:

- Locate the Check My Offer functionality.
- Select the correct commodity and district.
- Enter the correct offered price and unit.
- Complete the price-check process.
- Identify the resulting fairness classification.
- Understand the market reference and confidence label.

**Expected Outcome:**

The system should display an understandable price comparison result, provided sufficient verified market data is available.

The illustrative conversion for the scenario is:

P152 ÷ 20 kg = P7.60/kg.

The actual fairness classification must be determined using the verified market reference returned by the system. The test must not assume a classification without that reference.

### Task 2: Submit a Market Price

**Target User:** Market Vendor

**Scenario:**

A market vendor wants to contribute information about the selling price of tomatoes to Tsala Market.

**Participant Instructions:**

Using the vendor interface:

1. Open the price submission page.
2. Select Tomatoes.
3. Select the relevant district.
4. Select the appropriate quality grade.
5. Enter the price information.
6. Select the applicable price unit.
7. Review the normalised price preview.
8. Submit the price entry.
9. Identify the resulting submission status.

**Success Criteria:**

The participant should be able to:

- Locate the price submission form.
- Enter all required information.
- Understand the price-unit selection.
- Understand the normalised price preview.
- Submit the entry successfully.
- Identify whether the submission is pending verification or verified.

**Expected Outcome:**

The system should accept a valid submission and display its appropriate verification status.

### Task 3: Review a Price Comparison Result

**Target User:** Smallholder Farmer

**Scenario:**

A farmer has completed a price check and received a market comparison result. The farmer wants to understand whether the offered price is fair and what the displayed information means.

**Participant Instructions:**

1. Examine the displayed result.
2. Identify the fairness classification.
3. Identify the reference market price.
4. Identify the confidence label.
5. Explain what the result means.
6. Identify the disclaimer.

**Success Criteria:**

The participant should be able to correctly explain:

- The meaning of the fairness classification.
- The difference between the offered price and reference price.
- The meaning of the confidence label.
- Why the result is indicative rather than a guaranteed selling price.

### 8.1 Example of a Possible Design Revision

The following is an illustrative example, not an actual test finding.

**Problem:** A participant has difficulty understanding the price-unit selection.

**Original Design:** The interface displays different price units without explaining their meaning.

**Proposed Revision:** Add short descriptions beside each price unit, such as:

- Per kilogram (kg).
- Per crate.
- Per bag.

**Reason:** Clearer unit descriptions help users select the correct unit and reduce incorrect price conversions.

**Verification:** Ask a participant to repeat the price submission task and observe whether they select the correct unit without assistance.

## 11. Conclusion

The usability evaluation provides an opportunity to assess whether the Tsala Market prototype supports the needs of its intended users.

By involving three participants and observing their interactions with the proposed interface, the team can identify usability problems that may not be apparent during design.

The findings will guide improvements to the farmer and vendor workflows, navigation structure, interface instructions and accessibility features.

The revised prototype will be evaluated against the identified usability problems to determine whether the proposed changes improve task completion and overall user experience.
